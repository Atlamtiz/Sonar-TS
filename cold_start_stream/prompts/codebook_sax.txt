## CODEBOOK of the symbolic column `sym`   (symbolization: SAX)

USE IT FOR every question about the FORM of the curve: rises, falls, flat stretches,
peaks, dips, plateaus, steps, calm vs. choppy stretches, and sequences of these
("rises, then stays flat, then falls"). When a question describes a shape or a
trend pattern, search with this column FIRST, then verify on raw data.

### 1. How the string is built

The window is cut into equal segments and the mean of each segment becomes one letter:
    daily_feature     24 letters, one per hour
    monthly_feature   28-31 letters, one per day
    yearly_feature    12 letters, one per month
Letters a < b < c < d < e are levels RELATIVE TO THE WINDOW ITSELF (z-scores of the
segment means within that window): a = far below the window's mean, c = near the
mean, e = far above it. sym_len is the number of letters. Letter i (0-based) covers one
segment (1 hour / 1 day / 1 month): in a complete window, the segment that begins i segments
after window_start. A window at the very start or end of the series can be incomplete: it
has fewer letters, and in the FIRST window of the series letter 0 is the segment of the first
timestamp of raw_data, not window_start.
Consequences:
- sym describes shape, not absolute level. An "e" in one window and an "e" in another
  window are different values. Compare levels across windows with avg_val / min_val /
  max_val, never with letters.
- Every window is stretched to its own scale, so a window in which almost nothing
  happens still shows letters from a to e (noise looks like shape). Combine sym with
  std_val or (max_val - min_val) to tell a real movement from noise.
- A perfectly constant window is all "c".

### 2. How shapes look

    rise             letters climb from left to right          "aabcdee"   "bbcde"
    fall             letters descend                           "eedcbaa"
    flat / stable    a run of one letter, or of two neighbours, AT ANY LEVEL: a flat
                     stretch after a rise sits high ("abcdeeeee"), after a fall it sits
                     low ("edcbaaaaa"), in an uneventful window it sits in the middle
                     ("cccccc", "dcddcd")
    fast change      (reading hint) the climb or descent takes few letters   "aaaeee"
    slow change      (reading hint) it takes many letters                    "aabbccdde"
    peak / spike     low, briefly high, low again              "bbbebbb"   "aacecaa"
    dip / valley     high, briefly low, high again             "dddaddd"
    high plateau     a long run of d / e between lower letters "bbeeeeeebb"
    low plateau      a long run of a / b between higher letters "ddaaaaaadd"
    step up / down   a low run then a high run (or the reverse) "aaaaaeeeee" "eeeeebaaaa"
    calm vs. choppy  (reading hint) few letter changes vs. many back-and-forth
                                                               "cccdcccc"  "aecbdaeb"
A sequence of shapes is the patterns of its parts, in the order the question gives.

### 3. How to search

Two SQL functions are registered on `conn` (Python `re.search` semantics), in addition
to LIKE and GLOB:  `sym REGEXP ?`  and  `regexp_like(sym, ?)`.

    SELECT window_start, window_end, sym, slope, std_val FROM daily_feature
     WHERE channel_id = ? AND window_start >= ? AND window_start < ?
       AND sym REGEXP ?

Writing patterns:
- put only the DIRECTIONS and their ORDER into the pattern (rise / fall / flat, peak /
  dip). Do NOT encode words of degree (rapid, slow, steady, fluctuating, large, small)
  in the pattern: on real, noisy data a slow rise is not a clean staircase, and a
  pattern that insists on one loses true answers. Degree is measured on raw data in
  the verify step;
- use letter classes for tolerance: low = [ab], high = [de], middle = [bcd];
- a rise is "low letters somewhere before high letters":  [ab].*[de]   (a fall is the reverse);
- a flat part is a run of at least n equal letters at WHATEVER level the previous part
  ended:  a{n,}|b{n,}|c{n,}|d{n,}|e{n,}   with n = the duration you need, counted in
  letters of the table you query. Never assume that a flat part sits at "c";
- join the parts of a multi-part shape with ".*" in the order of the question.
Keep the pattern LOOSE. sym is an approximation: the search must not lose true answers
(recall first); exactness comes from the verify step. You can also read the sym strings
into Python and use `re.finditer` to get the POSITION of a match; position i maps back
to time through the letter rule above, which gives a first estimate of where inside
the window the shape starts and ends.

### 4. Choosing the table

Pick the table by the DURATION OF THE SHAPE ITSELF, never by the span of the question's
time range: a shape lasting hours -> daily_feature (1 letter = 1 hour); lasting days ->
monthly_feature (1 letter = 1 day); lasting months -> yearly_feature. "Days during 2020
that show a rise, then a fall" asks for shapes INSIDE single days, so it is daily_feature
searched over the year 2020 — the year is the search range, not the shape's duration. A shape must span at least 2-3 letters to
be visible. A shape can straddle a window border: when that matters, also look at the
neighbouring windows, or verify on a raw slice that extends beyond the window.
